# Title: Predicting Molecular Toxicity from Structure Alone: Mutagenicity, Cardiac Liability and Liver Injury on the TDC ADMET Benchmark

## Keywords
molecular property prediction, toxicity, AMES mutagenicity, hERG inhibition, drug-induced liver injury, SMILES, scaffold split, AUROC

## TL;DR
Given only the SMILES strings of small molecules and labelled training data, how well can three binary toxicity endpoints (AMES mutagenicity, hERG cardiac liability, drug-induced liver injury) be predicted under a scaffold split, and which representation and modelling choices matter? Investigate this on the TDC ADMET toxicity benchmark.

## Abstract
Early toxicity screening is one of the most consequential prediction problems in drug discovery: a compound that is mutagenic, blocks the hERG potassium channel, or injures the liver is usually abandoned regardless of its potency. The TDC ADMET toxicity benchmark provides three such endpoints as binary labels on drug-like molecules: AMES (mutagenicity, several thousand molecules), hERG (cardiac ion-channel liability, a few hundred molecules) and DILI (drug-induced liver injury, a few hundred molecules). Every molecule is given only as a SMILES string together with its endpoint identity. The public release provides a labelled training split and a labelled validation split, both produced by a scaffold split so that validation molecules come from chemotypes unseen in training, plus an unlabelled evaluation split whose labels are held back for final scoring. Performance on each endpoint is measured by AUROC, and the headline number is the unweighted mean of the three endpoint AUROCs; the validation split is scored with the provided official validation scorer, while the held-back split is scored by the organisers on the submitted probabilities. The scientific questions are what representation of a SMILES string (character n-grams, hand-computed substructure counts, learned embeddings, or anything else) carries toxicity signal that survives a scaffold shift, whether the three endpoints should share a model or be modelled separately, how the very small hERG and DILI training sets should be handled, and how much of the gap to a strong baseline is closed by each choice. Contributions typically compare two to four materially different configurations under identical conditions, each with exactly one validation-evaluated candidate, and report validation AUROC per endpoint together with the compute they spend. The computational setting is self-contained: work runs offline on CPU with the provided splits, numpy, pandas, scipy, scikit-learn and PyTorch, without RDKit and without downloads.
